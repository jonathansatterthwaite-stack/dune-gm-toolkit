# Dune GM Toolkit

A desktop app for running **Dune: Adventures in the Imperium** (Modiphius, 2d20) on Windows: characters, lore and the GM's tools in one window, plus a player display for a second screen.

**The layout works like Blender's.** The only thing above the work is a thin strip of workspace tabs; below it the window is divided into **areas**, and each area shows one **editor**, picked from the menu at the left of its header:

| Group | Editors |
|---|---|
| **Characters** | Characters (guided creation, the character editor and the list), Character sheet |
| **Lore** | Compendium, Entry, Timeline, Known universe, Event details, Galaxy map, World view, System details, Worlds, House |
| **GM** | Controls (Threat, Momentum, the session's buttons and the toolkit's settings), Dice, Conflict, NPCs, Scene, Hooks, Reference, Player display |

- **Resize** areas by dragging the border between them (double-click a border to even it out).
- **Split** an area from its **⋮** menu (left and right, or top and bottom); the new area starts on an editor that isn't showing yet.
- **Join** areas: right-click a border and keep one side, or use **Take over neighbor's space** or **Close this area** from the ⋮ menu. The border's menu also swaps the two sides or turns a side-by-side pair into a stack.
- **Maximize** the area under the pointer with **Ctrl+Space** (or ⤢ in its header), and the same again to go back.
- An editor shows once per workspace: picking one that's already in another area swaps the two.

**Workspaces** are the tabs in that strip, each with its own arrangement: **Session** (Controls, Dice, Scene, Conflict, NPCs, Character sheet, Player display, Reference), **Prep** (Compendium and Entry, NPCs, Hooks, Worlds, House), **Lore** (Galaxy map, Compendium and Entry, with the Timeline across the bottom between Known universe and Event details), **Galaxy** (the Galaxy map with World view and System details beside it) and **Characters** (Characters, Compendium and Entry). Press **+** to add one (a copy of the current one), double-click a tab to rename it, and right-click it to duplicate, reset or delete it. Everything is saved as you go.

**The desktop app has no menu bar or separate title bar:** the strip of tabs is the title bar (drag it to move the window, double-click it to maximize), with the window buttons at its right end. **Toolkit** in the Controls editor has the theme (Imperial or Parchment), the data folder, importing browser data and resetting workspaces. Keys: F5 reloads, F11 is full screen, Ctrl+= and Ctrl+− zoom (Ctrl+0 resets), F12 opens the developer tools.

**Editors work together.** A Compendium NPC goes to the roster or the conflict with one click, a talent to the character you have open, a skill on a character sheet to the dice roller, a hazard to the scene. When the editor that should show the result isn't on screen, the toolkit switches to a workspace that has it (or, if none does, puts it in the largest area).

**The player display** is a second window for the players, on a projector, TV or second monitor. Open it full screen on the screen you pick from the **Player display** editor, and choose what's on it:
- **A background:** a plain Imperial screen, the **galaxy map**, or the **timeline**. The map follows your Galaxy map: pick a system or world there, or open a solar system, and the display flies to it, slowly orbiting, with the name in large type. The timeline shows your campaign's timeline and glides to the event or year you pick in the Timeline, but never past the **players' year** (set it from the timeline's header; it starts at the RPG's 10190 AG), so nothing later is spoiled.
- **Cards** on top, up to four at once: **Show to players** on a Compendium entry (with its art), a character sheet, an NPC card, or a **handout** you type in. An NPC's card shows only their name, title and faction, never stats, notes or the book's summary.
- **Overlays:** Momentum and Threat, the scene's traits and goal tasks, the conflict's zones with who stands in each, and each dice roll as it's made (player rolls only, unless you include NPC rolls). **Black out** hides it all at once.

With only one screen, the display opens as a window you can move and resize.

**Your data** is in `Documents\Dune GM\toolkit.json`. Copy the folder to back up or move a campaign; **Open data folder** (Controls › Toolkit) takes you there. The first time the toolkit runs, it combines the data of the three earlier apps (`character.json`, `lore.json` and `gm.json` in that folder) into `toolkit.json`, and leaves those files as they were.

## Building the app

Needs [Node.js](https://nodejs.org/) (22 or newer).

```bash
npm install
npm run build
```

This writes `Dune GM Toolkit.exe` to `desktop\dist\`. It's portable: no installer, and it runs from any folder. If you've built the local book text and art (below), they're included in the exe, so it's for your own use; don't share it.

While working on the code, run it straight from the source with `npm start`, or open `index.html` in a browser (it then keeps its data in the browser's storage, and `display.html` is the player display). The pages are generated: edit `desktop/template.html`, then run `node tools/make-pages.js`, and bump the `?v=` numbers in the template when you change a CSS or JS file. For self-tests, `DUNE_GM_DATA=<folder>` points the app at a scratch data folder, `DUNE_EVAL=<script>` runs a script in the page and saves its result and a screenshot, and `DUNE_EVAL_DISPLAY=<script>` does the same in the player display.

**Moving from the old browser version:** open `export-browser-data.html` in the browser you used, save the file, then choose **Import browser data…** (Controls › Toolkit). The website version (GitHub Pages) has been retired.

## What's in it

| Tab | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Controls** | Shows the Threat and Momentum pools. Momentum is capped at 6, and anything over the cap is lost with a warning. It also has Undo, End scene (−1 Momentum, and Resist Defeat refreshes) and New session (sets starting Threat from the House type × number of players). Under **Toolkit ▾**: the theme, the data folder, importing browser data, and resetting this or every workspace. |
| **Dice** | Rolls a full skill test: Skill + Drive, focus crits, Difficulty 0–5+, complication range 1–5, and up to 3 bonus d20 (paid with Momentum, Threat, or a mix). It covers the Determination auto-1 and re-roll, and assistants who add 1d20 each. It shows live odds of passing, then lets you bank or spend Momentum, buy off complications, and chain a contest. NPC mode pays from Threat. Press **R** to roll. |
| **Conflict** | Tracks each side's combatants, defeat tracks (skill + Quality of defensive assets), hits (2 + asset Quality, boosted with Momentum), and Resist Defeat (1 + Quality). It also handles Keep the Initiative, rounds, assets, complications, zones, and +3 minor NPCs at once. |
| **NPCs** | Generates Minor, Notable and Major NPCs by the Core rules (p.137, p.240) from 35 roles, including the Power and Pawns outsiders (Face Dancer, Ginaz swordmaster, Tleilaxu, Ixian, courtier, CHOAM). It can optionally swap in one of the six Power and Pawns drives. Save NPCs to a roster, roll for them, or drop them into a conflict. |
| **Characters** | A guided character creator for player characters (planned or quick-start), supporting characters (minor and notable, with their Momentum costs) and NPCs (minor, notable, major). It walks through concept, faction template, archetype, skills, focuses, talents, drives and statements, assets and finishing touches, and checks every creation rule live, telling you exactly what's left. It covers 32 archetypes (Core, *Sand and Dust* Fremen, *The Great Game* Guild), 10 faction templates with their mandatory talents, 153 talents with short summaries (every talent in the four books), *Power and Pawns* drives, and NPC special abilities. "Auto-fill the rest" completes any character with valid picks. The live sheet lets you click a skill to roll it on the Dice tab, add the character to a conflict, or print it. Generated NPCs open in the creator with one click. The sheet can also have an area of its own, the **Character sheet** editor: a row of buttons switches character (players first), and clicking a skill row fills in the Dice, so it sits in the Session workspace for play. While it is showing, the Characters editor leaves out its own sheet. |
| **Guided character creation** | On the Characters tab, **Guided character creation** walks a player through making a character one screen at a time: Concept, Faction, Archetype, Skills, Focuses, Talents, Drives, Assets, Finishing touches and Review, either as a planned character (Core p.108) or created in play (Core p.132). Faction templates and archetypes are laid out like the book: a sideways-scrolling strip of entries, each with diamond-bulleted trait, skills, focuses, talents and drives. The archetypes are grouped under the book's headings as tabs down the left (Warmasters, Socialites, Disciples, Adepts, Academics), with an "at a glance" table. The Fremen archetypes (*Sand and Dust*) are offered only to a Fremen character and the Guild archetypes (*The Great Game*) only to a Spacing Guild one; if the faction changes afterwards, the Archetype step says so. Each screen explains the step, checks the rules as you go, and the step bar shows what's done. The character is saved to the Characters list as you build it, and you can open it in the full editor at any time. The Faction and Archetype screens fit the window (or the area) exactly, so they only scroll sideways: the strip takes the height left under the heading, Back/Next sit at the bottom, every card in a strip has the same height and width (archetype cards widen until the longest text fits), and the mouse wheel scrolls the strip sideways. In a narrow area the archetype tabs become a row. Faction template cards are tall like the book's faction pages and always use the book's light parchment (in the dark theme too, so the art cut from the page sits on the colour it was printed on), with the character as a faint watermark behind the text, scaled to the card's full height. House Agent and Ginaz Cadet have no portrait. When the book text has been built locally, the cards use the book's own descriptions, and `python tools/extract-art.py` (needs `pip install pypdfium2 pillow`) pulls the books' art from your PDFs into `img/local/art/` so the faction cards (and the Compendium) show it. Both are gitignored and only load on your own computer; the public site shows a drawn emblem instead. |
| **Scene** | Holds the scene's traits (with ranks), example complications, notes, and extended tasks and peril clocks. |
| **House** | Runs *The Great Game*'s House management. The House sheet covers status level and its effects, Wealth, Resources, House skills, domains, spaces, roles, upkeep levels, holdings, traits and boons. A yearly session walks through news, income, events by status level, upkeep, ventures and end of year. Ventures roll House skill + the leader's drive, pay the cost, adjust status, and carry partial successes into next year. Every step is recorded in a House chronicle. |
| **Worlds** | Generators for planets and rival Houses (*The Great Game*), Fremen sietches (*Sand and Dust*), and desert terrain and hazards. A rival's skills can be rolled as House actions, hazards can be added to the scene, and a worm clock can be started on the Scene tab. |
| **Galaxy map** | A 3D map of the Known Universe, built with three.js (maximize its area with Ctrl+Space, or ⛶ for the whole screen). It shows a procedural spiral galaxy, and you zoom from the whole galaxy into the Imperium around Sol. About 20 canon systems are placed from Dune lore. Where a world orbits a real star (Arrakis/Canopus, Caladan/Delta Pavonis, Giedi Prime/36 Ophiuchi B, Kaitain/Alpha Piscium, Ix/Alkalurops, Ecaz/Alpha Centauri B and more), it sits at that star's real position. Worlds around invented stars get estimated positions, and each system is labelled "Real star" or "Estimated position". Click a star or search to fly there: the planet renders in a panel on the left and its details open on the right. Hovering shows distances from Sol and from the selected system. Each planet's look is editable, with 14 planet types, colours, sea level, ice, clouds, atmosphere, tilt, spin, moons, dunes, craters, gas bands, storms, city lights, lava and rings. You can save any look as your own type, and add systems and worlds. **Solar systems:** press "View solar system" (or double-click a star) to open the system. Its star sits at the centre, sized and coloured by spectral class, and lights its worlds as they move on animated orbits. Lore orbit numerals place each world (Arrakis III, Wallach IX), roughly following the Titius–Bode pattern (III ≈ 1 AU). Year lengths come from Kepler's law, using the star's mass. Empty orbits get stable, generated uncharted worlds (hot rocks inside, gas giants outside, sometimes an asteroid belt), and any of them can be charted with one click. Click a world to follow it; double-click empty space for the whole system. Time controls let you pause or run orbits at ×¼ to ×16. Orbit spacing is compressed for display. Press Esc to back out one level. Its two panels can also have areas of their own: **World view** (the spinning world and its caption) and **System details** (the system, its worlds and their look). The Galaxy workspace has them beside the map; while one is showing, the map leaves out that floating panel. |
| **Hooks** | Generates adventure seeds. Click any part of a seed to re-roll just that part. |
| **Compendium** | A searchable database of the four books (about 900 entries in 18 tables): 127 assets (weapons, gear, troops, vehicles, poisons, agents, leverage) with keywords and Quality; 73 NPC stat blocks (the named characters plus every archetype in the Core rules, *Power and Pawns* and *Sand and Dust*); every talent, archetype and faction template; 71 noble Houses with their traits, domains, homeworlds, households and vassals; 35 planets; House creation tables (types, areas of expertise, roles, rival House templates, enemies and hatred); House ventures; drives; focuses; factions; and traits. Search everything at once, filter by book, and follow links between entries (a House to its homeworld, an NPC to its talents). Entries hand off to the other tools: save an NPC to the roster, drop it into a conflict or open it in the creator; give an asset or talent to the current character; add a trait to the scene; show a planet on the galaxy map. Add your own **homebrew** entries to any table, and export everything as JSON or a table as CSV. The picked entry can have an area of its own, the **Entry** editor (the built-in workspaces have it beside the Compendium): while it is showing, the Compendium is the search and the list, and the entry links in the timeline open there. In a narrow area the tables are a dropdown. |
| **Compendium: full book text** (your computer only) | Run `node tools/build-fulltext.js` once (needs Node and `pdftotext`, and your PDFs in `[rulebooks]/`). It writes `js/db/fulltext.local.js`, and the Compendium then shows the books' own text under **From the book** on each entry, adds a **Book pages** table with every page of all four books (read page by page, or search it), and offers "Search the book text too". The file is gitignored and is only loaded when you open the toolkit from your own computer (double-clicked or on localhost), so the public site never has it. Re-run the script after adding database entries. |
| **Timeline** | Fills its area like the Galaxy map. Every timeline is a line across it: "Dune (the novels)" runs straight through, and each alternate timeline forks off the line it splits from at the point where its history changes, then runs alongside with its own events. "Agents of Dune" forks just before the Atreides would get Arrakis, because in that adventure the Emperor gives it to House Nagara. Scroll left and right with the mouse wheel, by dragging, with ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys; era bands run behind the lines, and long empty stretches are shrunk and labeled ("3,509 years"). Click a line (or its name) to follow that timeline: its history lights up, its own line plus its parents' lines up to each fork. Click a dot to read the event in the **Details** panel on the right (an event on another line follows that line). Each event's symbol shows its kind: birth, death, ghola, character, House, throne, world, war, intrigue, founding, discovery, faith, spice & trade, or galactic. The main characters' births and deaths are on the novels' line, along with each time someone returns as a ghola (Duncan Idaho as Hayt and Leto II's Duncans, Miles Teg, and the gholas of Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson's sequels); each event lists the sources its year comes from: Dune's Appendix IV, the Core rulebook's timeline (p.27), Wikipedia and the novels. Where they disagree, the novels' year is used and the event says what the others give. **Key** lists them with counts and shows or hides each kind; your own events pick a kind in the event form, or leave it on Automatic to have it guessed from the title, details and links. Your own events have a dashed ring. The year under the center line drives the rest of the tool: the **Year** panel on the left shows the era, that year's events and every change in effect, and the Compendium's entries and list columns show that year (turn this off with **Compendium follows this year**). Start your own alternate timeline with **Branch from here** on any event, or from a chosen year in the Details panel. A branch shares its parent's history up to the fork, and after it only its own events apply. Events can change entries from their date on: a planet's ruling House, a character's status. The **Year** and **Details** panels can also have areas of their own: the **Known universe** and **Event details** editors (the Lore workspace has them either side of the Timeline). While one of them is showing, the timeline leaves out its floating panel; maximize the Timeline and the floating panels come back. |
| **Reference** | Searchable rules summaries with book and page numbers: Core, *The Great Game*, *Power and Pawns* and *Sand and Dust*. |

Rules numbers (costs, bands, dice), keywords and stat blocks follow the books, with page references on every database entry. Talent, asset and other descriptions are short summaries written for this tool, not the books' text. Descriptions and the planet, sietch, hazard, name and hook tables are written for this tool, so check the books for the official tables.

## Files

- `index.html` (the toolkit) and `display.html` (the player display) are generated from `desktop/template.html` by `tools/make-pages.js`.
- `js/areas.js` is the layout: workspaces, areas, splitting, joining, resizing, maximizing, and bringing an editor into view when another asks for it (`S.tab`).
- `css/styles.css` handles the look: the dark Imperial theme by default, with a light Parchment theme.
- `js/cast.js` is the player display: the **Player display** editor, **Show to players**, and the display window itself (`display.html`), which reads the toolkit's data live and saves nothing.
- `js/data.js` holds the Core rules tables, NPC roles, names and hook tables.
- `js/expansions.js` holds the expansion data: House management costs and tables, planet, sietch and desert tables, court drives and outsider roles.
- `js/dice.js` is the dice engine and odds calculator. It doesn't touch the page.
- `js/app.js` is the core app and draws the main tabs. It exposes `window.GM` so other modules can add tabs.
- `js/house.js` is the House tab.
- `js/characters-data.js` holds archetypes, faction templates, talents and creation rules by character kind.
- `js/characters.js` is the Characters tab: the guided creator, rule checks, auto-fill, sheet and printing.
- `js/chargen.js` is the guided, multi-screen character creator. It reuses the Characters tab's rules, checks and auto-fill through `GM.chars`.
- `js/worlds.js` is the Worlds tab.
- `js/planets.js` holds the planet types, the procedural texture painter and the reusable 3D planet model.
- `js/galaxy-data.js` holds the canon star systems: real-star coordinates, estimated positions and lore notes.
- `js/galaxy.js` is the galaxy map: the galaxy scene, camera, panels, search and planet viewer.
- `js/db.js` is the rules database engine (`window.DUNE_DB`): tables, ids, search and views over data the other tools already hold.
- `js/db/*.js` are the database tables: `assets-core.js` and `assets-x.js` (assets), `npcs-core.js` and `npcs-x.js` (NPC stat blocks), `houses.js`, `planets.js`, and `rules.js` (House creation, roles, enmity, factions, traits, focuses, drives and the views). `adventures.js` holds the published adventures: the Agents of Dune starter set (with its pregenerated characters, cards and folios), the Masters of Dune campaign and the GM Toolkit. It adds their NPCs, assets (including Masters of Dune's spaceships and stations), House Nagara and its worlds, the cards' traits and complications, and an **Adventures** table of acts, chapters, campaign rules, intrigues and locations, each listing its characters. `lore.js` holds lore notes from the Dune novels, summarized from the [Dune Wiki](https://dune.fandom.com/). They appear in the Compendium under **In the novels**, labeled by canon (Frank Herbert's novels, the Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson novels, or the non-canon Dune Encyclopedia), and are flagged where they differ from the RPG books. They never change the RPG's own values.
- `css/imperial.css` is the Imperial theme, the default look: smoky charcoal with gold line work. It has pointed-arch frames for character art, stepped corner pieces on cards, a pointed frame on the open tab, eight-point stars for the timeline's events and dividers, and blue-and-gold cloud accents. All of it is original SVG line work, generated by `python tools/gen-imperial.py`. The **Parchment** button in the top bar switches to the original light theme; `js/theme.js` remembers the choice.
- `desktop/main.js` and `desktop/preload.js` are the Electron shell: the window, the data file (and combining the three earlier apps' files), the menus, and the player display's window on the screen the GM picks. `desktop/build.js` builds the exe.
- `js/tools.js` lists the editors in their groups (Characters, Lore, GM) and the saved data each group keeps; `js/store.js` loads and saves it. `GM.remote` carries out a change to another group's data through the handlers in `GM.inbox`.
- `js/compendium.js` is the Compendium tab: browsing, search, links between entries, hand-offs to the other tools, homebrew and export.
- `js/timeline.js` is the Timeline tab and the timeline engine: which events a branch sees, and an entry "as of" a year. `js/db/timeline.js` holds the eras, the built-in timelines and their events. The novels' years come from the Dune Wiki's timeline of Frank Herbert's novels, and events note where the RPG books give a different year. Your own timelines and events are saved with the rest of the app's state.
- `tools/build-fulltext.js` builds `js/db/fulltext.local.js` from your PDFs (the four rulebooks, Agents of Dune with its cards, folios, handouts, trackers and zone maps, Masters of Dune, the GM Toolkit and the GM Screen): it puts each page into reading order (splitting two- and three-column layouts), matches every database entry to its section by page reference and heading, and stores every page as text. Personal use only; the output is gitignored.
- `tools/audit-db.js` checks the database: run `node tools/audit-db.js` after editing any table. It loads the same scripts the page does and reports any row that breaks the house style, and exits with an error if it finds one. The house style is:
  - page references read `<Book> p.N`;
  - US spelling, as the books use;
  - Houses are always named "House X", and House ranks are Great, Major, Minor, Imperial or Unranked;
  - House domains are written "Area (Type): detail";
  - every talent, focus, archetype, House, homeworld and planet ruler that one entry names exists as an entry of its own.
- `tools/extract-art.py` cuts the books' art out of your PDFs (with transparency) into `img/local/art/` and writes `js/db/art.local.js`. That's 87 images: 37 character figures, 25 portrait busts (the Agents of Dune folios and character cards), 19 House banners and 6 crests. The manifest adds an **Art** table to the Compendium and links each image to the entries it shows: NPCs, faction templates, archetypes and Houses then carry their picture, and the creator's faction cards use it too. Most images are separate objects in the PDF. The five Power and Pawns court NPCs are flattened into their pages, and the Agents of Dune card portraits sit on a parchment background, so those are cut out by color (this needs `pip install numpy scipy`). Art printed more than once is kept once, at its sharpest, with the other pages listed. Personal use only; both are gitignored.
- `js/vendor/three.min.js` is three.js r187, bundled so it loads without a server. `js/vendor/README.md` explains how to rebuild it.
- `[rulebooks]/` contains your PDFs, which the rules were checked against.
