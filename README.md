# Dune GM Toolkit

Three desktop apps for **Dune: Adventures in the Imperium** (Modiphius, 2d20), built for Windows desktops:

| App | Tabs | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **Dune Character Maker** | Guided character creation, the character editor and the character list | Characters |
| **Dune Lore** | Compendium, Timeline, Galaxy map, Worlds, House | The database's homebrew, timelines, the galaxy map, generated worlds, House management |
| **Dune GM Tools** | Dice, Conflict, NPCs, Scene, Hooks, Reference | Threat and Momentum, rolls and the log, the conflict, the NPC roster, the scene, saved hooks |

**They work together.** All three read each other's data live: Lore's Compendium can give a talent to the character open in the Character maker, GM tools see the characters and the timeline. But each app edits only its own data. When one app needs a change in another (Lore's **Save to NPC roster**, a character's **Roll** button, a hazard sent to the scene), it sends a request and the owning app makes the change. It opens or comes to the front if needed, and the request waits until it is running. Switch apps with the buttons in the header or **Tools** in the menu (Ctrl+1, 2, 3).

**Your data** is in `Documents\Dune GM\`: `character.json`, `lore.json` and `gm.json`, plus an `inbox` folder for requests between the apps. Copy the folder to back up or move a campaign; **File › Open data folder** takes you there.

## Building the apps

Needs [Node.js](https://nodejs.org/) (22 or newer).

```bash
npm install
npm run build
```

This writes `Dune Character Maker.exe`, `Dune Lore.exe` and `Dune GM Tools.exe` to `desktop\dist\`. They're portable: no installer, and they can run from any folder, but keep the three together so each can open the others. If you've built the local book text and art (below), it's included in the exes, so they're for your own use; don't share them.

While working on the code, run an app straight from the source with `npm run character`, `npm run lore` or `npm run gm`, or open `character.html`, `lore.html` or `gm.html` in a browser (they then share their data through the browser's storage). The pages are generated: edit `desktop/template.html` or `js/tools.js`, then run `node tools/make-pages.js`.

**Moving from the old browser version:** open `export-browser-data.html` in the browser you used, save the file, then choose **File › Import browser data…** in any of the apps. The website version (GitHub Pages) has been retired.

## What's in it

| Tab | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Header** (GM tools) | Shows the Threat and Momentum pools. Momentum is capped at 6, and anything over the cap is lost with a warning. It also has Undo, End scene (−1 Momentum, and Resist Defeat refreshes) and New session (sets starting Threat from the House type × number of players). |
| **Dice** | Rolls a full skill test: Skill + Drive, focus crits, Difficulty 0–5+, complication range 1–5, and up to 3 bonus d20 (paid with Momentum, Threat, or a mix). It covers the Determination auto-1 and re-roll, and assistants who add 1d20 each. It shows live odds of passing, then lets you bank or spend Momentum, buy off complications, and chain a contest. NPC mode pays from Threat. Press **R** to roll. |
| **Conflict** | Tracks each side's combatants, defeat tracks (skill + Quality of defensive assets), hits (2 + asset Quality, boosted with Momentum), and Resist Defeat (1 + Quality). It also handles Keep the Initiative, rounds, assets, complications, zones, and +3 minor NPCs at once. |
| **NPCs** | Generates Minor, Notable and Major NPCs by the Core rules (p.137, p.240) from 35 roles, including the Power and Pawns outsiders (Face Dancer, Ginaz swordmaster, Tleilaxu, Ixian, courtier, CHOAM). It can optionally swap in one of the six Power and Pawns drives. Save NPCs to a roster, roll for them, or drop them into a conflict. |
| **Characters** | A guided character creator for player characters (planned or quick-start), supporting characters (minor and notable, with their Momentum costs) and NPCs (minor, notable, major). It walks through concept, faction template, archetype, skills, focuses, talents, drives and statements, assets and finishing touches, and checks every creation rule live, telling you exactly what's left. It covers 32 archetypes (Core, *Sand and Dust* Fremen, *The Great Game* Guild), 10 faction templates with their mandatory talents, 153 talents with short summaries (every talent in the four books), *Power and Pawns* drives, and NPC special abilities. "Auto-fill the rest" completes any character with valid picks. The live sheet lets you click a skill to roll it on the Dice tab, add the character to a conflict, or print it. Generated NPCs open in the creator with one click. |
| **Guided character creation** | On the Characters tab, **Guided character creation** walks a player through making a character one screen at a time: Concept, Archetype, Skills, Focuses, Talents, Drives, Assets, Finishing touches and Review, either as a planned character (Core p.108) or created in play (Core p.132). Faction templates and archetypes are laid out like the book: a sideways-scrolling strip of entries grouped under the book's headings (Warmasters, Socialites, Disciples, Adepts, Academics, plus the Fremen and Guild archetypes), each with diamond-bulleted trait, skills, focuses, talents and drives, and an "at a glance" table. Each screen explains the step, checks the rules as you go, and the step bar shows what's done. The character is saved to the Characters list as you build it, and you can open it in the full editor at any time. On a desktop screen (1000×620 or larger) the wizard uses the full window width, each strip grows to fill the window under the header with Back/Next pinned below, cards widen until their text fits, and once a strip is fully on screen the mouse wheel scrolls it sideways (at either end, or when the strip is only partly visible, the wheel scrolls the page as usual). Faction template cards are tall like the book's faction pages, with the character standing under the text. When the book text has been built locally, the cards use the book's own descriptions, and `python tools/extract-art.py` (needs `pip install pypdfium2 pillow`) pulls the books' art from your PDFs into `img/local/art/` so the faction cards (and the Compendium) show it. Both are gitignored and only load on your own computer; the public site shows a drawn emblem instead. |
| **Scene** | Holds the scene's traits (with ranks), example complications, notes, and extended tasks and peril clocks. |
| **House** | Runs *The Great Game*'s House management. The House sheet covers status level and its effects, Wealth, Resources, House skills, domains, spaces, roles, upkeep levels, holdings, traits and boons. A yearly session walks through news, income, events by status level, upkeep, ventures and end of year. Ventures roll House skill + the leader's drive, pay the cost, adjust status, and carry partial successes into next year. Every step is recorded in a House chronicle. |
| **Worlds** | Generators for planets and rival Houses (*The Great Game*), Fremen sietches (*Sand and Dust*), and desert terrain and hazards. A rival's skills can be rolled as House actions, hazards can be added to the scene, and a worm clock can be started on the Scene tab. |
| **Galaxy map** | A full-screen 3D map of the Known Universe, built with three.js. It shows a procedural spiral galaxy, and you zoom from the whole galaxy into the Imperium around Sol. About 20 canon systems are placed from Dune lore. Where a world orbits a real star (Arrakis/Canopus, Caladan/Delta Pavonis, Giedi Prime/36 Ophiuchi B, Kaitain/Alpha Piscium, Ix/Alkalurops, Ecaz/Alpha Centauri B and more), it sits at that star's real position. Worlds around invented stars get estimated positions, and each system is labelled "Real star" or "Estimated position". Click a star or search to fly there: the planet renders in a panel on the left and its details open on the right. Hovering shows distances from Sol and from the selected system. Each planet's look is editable, with 14 planet types, colours, sea level, ice, clouds, atmosphere, tilt, spin, moons, dunes, craters, gas bands, storms, city lights, lava and rings. You can save any look as your own type, and add systems and worlds. **Solar systems:** press "View solar system" (or double-click a star) to open the system. Its star sits at the centre, sized and coloured by spectral class, and lights its worlds as they move on animated orbits. Lore orbit numerals place each world (Arrakis III, Wallach IX), roughly following the Titius–Bode pattern (III ≈ 1 AU). Year lengths come from Kepler's law, using the star's mass. Empty orbits get stable, generated uncharted worlds (hot rocks inside, gas giants outside, sometimes an asteroid belt), and any of them can be charted with one click. Click a world to follow it; double-click empty space for the whole system. Time controls let you pause or run orbits at ×¼ to ×16. Orbit spacing is compressed for display. Press Esc to back out one level. |
| **Hooks** | Generates adventure seeds. Click any part of a seed to re-roll just that part. |
| **Compendium** | A searchable database of the four books (about 900 entries in 18 tables): 127 assets (weapons, gear, troops, vehicles, poisons, agents, leverage) with keywords and Quality; 73 NPC stat blocks (the named characters plus every archetype in the Core rules, *Power and Pawns* and *Sand and Dust*); every talent, archetype and faction template; 71 noble Houses with their traits, domains, homeworlds, households and vassals; 35 planets; House creation tables (types, areas of expertise, roles, rival House templates, enemies and hatred); House ventures; drives; focuses; factions; and traits. Search everything at once, filter by book, and follow links between entries (a House to its homeworld, an NPC to its talents). Entries hand off to the other tools: save an NPC to the roster, drop it into a conflict or open it in the creator; give an asset or talent to the current character; add a trait to the scene; show a planet on the galaxy map. Add your own **homebrew** entries to any table, and export everything as JSON or a table as CSV. |
| **Compendium: full book text** (your computer only) | Run `node tools/build-fulltext.js` once (needs Node and `pdftotext`, and your PDFs in `[rulebooks]/`). It writes `js/db/fulltext.local.js`, and the Compendium then shows the books' own text under **From the book** on each entry, adds a **Book pages** table with every page of all four books (read page by page, or search it), and offers "Search the book text too". The file is gitignored and is only loaded when you open the toolkit from your own computer (double-clicked or on localhost), so the public site never has it. Re-run the script after adding database entries. |
| **Timeline** | A horizontal timeline that scrolls left to right (mouse wheel, drag, ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys) with each event as a dot: pick one to read it. Era bands run behind the dots, and long empty stretches are shrunk and labeled ("3,509 years"). The year under the center line drives the rest of the tool. The panel below shows the era, that year's events and every change in effect, and the Compendium's entries and list columns show that year (turn this off with **Compendium follows this year**). "Dune (the novels)" holds the canon events; "Agents of Dune" splits off just before the Atreides would get Arrakis, because in that adventure the Emperor gives it to House Nagara. Start your own alternate timeline with **Branch from here** on any event, or from a chosen year. A branch shares its parent's history up to that point, and after it only its own events apply. Events can change entries from their date on: a planet's ruling House, a character's status. |
| **Reference** | Searchable rules summaries with book and page numbers: Core, *The Great Game*, *Power and Pawns* and *Sand and Dust*. |

Rules numbers (costs, bands, dice), keywords and stat blocks follow the books, with page references on every database entry. Talent, asset and other descriptions are short summaries written for this tool, not the books' text. Descriptions and the planet, sietch, hazard, name and hook tables are written for this tool, so check the books for the official tables.

## Files

- `index.html` is the page itself. When you change a CSS or JS file, bump the `?v=` number so browsers load the new copy.
- `css/styles.css` handles the look, with a light and a dark theme that follow your OS setting.
- `js/data.js` holds the Core rules tables, NPC roles, names and hook tables.
- `js/expansions.js` holds the expansion data: House management costs and tables, planet, sietch and desert tables, court drives and outsider roles.
- `js/dice.js` is the dice engine and odds calculator. It doesn't touch the page.
- `js/app.js` is the core app and draws the main tabs. It exposes `window.GM` so other modules can add tabs.
- `js/house.js` is the House tab.
- `js/characters-data.js` holds archetypes, faction templates, talents and creation rules by character kind.
- `js/characters.js` is the Characters tab: the guided creator, rule checks, auto-fill, sheet and printing.
- `js/chargen.js` is the guided, multi-screen character creator. It reuses the Characters tab's rules, checks and auto-fill through `GM.chars`.
- `js/worlds.js` is the Worlds tab.
- `js/planets.js` holds the planet types, the procedural texture painter and the reusable 3D planet model.
- `js/galaxy-data.js` holds the canon star systems: real-star coordinates, estimated positions and lore notes.
- `js/galaxy.js` is the galaxy map: the galaxy scene, camera, panels, search and planet viewer.
- `js/db.js` is the rules database engine (`window.DUNE_DB`): tables, ids, search and views over data the other tools already hold.
- `js/db/*.js` are the database tables: `assets-core.js` and `assets-x.js` (assets), `npcs-core.js` and `npcs-x.js` (NPC stat blocks), `houses.js`, `planets.js`, and `rules.js` (House creation, roles, enmity, factions, traits, focuses, drives and the views). `adventures.js` holds the published adventures: the Agents of Dune starter set (with its pregenerated characters, cards and folios), the Masters of Dune campaign and the GM Toolkit. It adds their NPCs, assets (including Masters of Dune's spaceships and stations), House Nagara and its worlds, the cards' traits and complications, and an **Adventures** table of acts, chapters, campaign rules, intrigues and locations, each listing its characters. `lore.js` holds lore notes from the Dune novels, summarized from the [Dune Wiki](https://dune.fandom.com/). They appear in the Compendium under **In the novels**, labeled by canon (Frank Herbert's novels, the Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson novels, or the non-canon Dune Encyclopedia), and are flagged where they differ from the RPG books. They never change the RPG's own values.
- `css/imperial.css` is the Imperial theme, the default look: smoky charcoal with gold line work. It has pointed-arch frames for character art, stepped corner pieces on cards, a pointed frame on the open tab, eight-point stars for the timeline's events and dividers, and blue-and-gold cloud accents. All of it is original SVG line work, generated by `python tools/gen-imperial.py`. The **Parchment** button in the top bar switches to the original light theme; `js/theme.js` remembers the choice.
- `desktop/main.js` and `desktop/preload.js` are the Electron shell: one codebase that runs as any of the three apps, the shared data files, the request inboxes and the menus. `desktop/build.js` builds the three exes.
- `js/tools.js` says which tabs and which saved data belong to each app; `js/store.js` loads and saves through it, and `GM.remote` sends requests to the owning app.
- `js/compendium.js` is the Compendium tab: browsing, search, links between entries, hand-offs to the other tools, homebrew and export.
- `js/timeline.js` is the Timeline tab and the timeline engine: which events a branch sees, and an entry "as of" a year. `js/db/timeline.js` holds the eras, the built-in timelines and their events. The novels' years come from the Dune Wiki's timeline of Frank Herbert's novels, and events note where the RPG books give a different year. Your own timelines and events are saved with the rest of the app's state.
- `tools/build-fulltext.js` builds `js/db/fulltext.local.js` from your PDFs (the four rulebooks, Agents of Dune with its cards, folios, handouts, trackers and zone maps, Masters of Dune, the GM Toolkit and the GM Screen): it puts each page into reading order (splitting two- and three-column layouts), matches every database entry to its section by page reference and heading, and stores every page as text. Personal use only; the output is gitignored.
- `tools/audit-db.js` checks the database: run `node tools/audit-db.js` after editing any table. It loads the same scripts the page does and reports any row that breaks the house style, and exits with an error if it finds one. The house style is:
  - page references read `<Book> p.N`;
  - US spelling, as the books use;
  - Houses are always named "House X", and House ranks are Great, Major, Minor, Imperial or Unranked;
  - House domains are written "Area (Type): detail";
  - every talent, focus, archetype, House, homeworld and planet ruler that one entry names exists as an entry of its own.
- `tools/extract-art.py` cuts the books' art out of your PDFs (with transparency) into `img/local/art/` and writes `js/db/art.local.js`. That's 87 images: 37 character figures, 25 portrait busts (the Agents of Dune folios and character cards), 19 House banners and 6 crests. The manifest adds an **Art** table to the Compendium and links each image to the entries it shows: NPCs, faction templates, archetypes and Houses then carry their picture, and the creator's faction cards use it too. Most images are separate objects in the PDF. The five Power and Pawns court NPCs are flattened into their pages, and the Agents of Dune card portraits sit on a parchment background, so those are cut out by color (this needs `pip install numpy scipy`). Art printed more than once is kept once, at its sharpest, with the other pages listed. Personal use only; both are gitignored.
- `js/vendor/three.min.js` is three.js r187, bundled so it loads without a server. `js/vendor/README.md` explains how to rebuild it.
- `[rulebooks]/` contains your PDFs, which the rules were checked against.
